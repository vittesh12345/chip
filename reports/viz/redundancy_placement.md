## What this shows

The routed DEF carries 5 placement fences (DEF REGIONS, TYPE FENCE: pdsep_copyA, pdsep_copyB, pdsep_th0, pdsep_th1, pdsep_th2) with their GROUPS; the pd flow (pd/sky130hd_sep/config.mk) created them to keep the redundant copies apart.

Across the 9 redundant groups the copies' centroids are 100.7-296.0 µm apart. Corresponding bits of different copies are at least 97.92 µm apart centre-to-centre (median of the group medians 291.5 µm); 0 of 262 same-bit pairs sit in cells that touch.

In the unconstrained baseline the closest same-bit pair was 2.76 µm centre to centre and 13 of 262 same-bit pairs touched, so one particle track or charge-sharing event could upset both copies of a bit. With the fences no copy pair touches and the copies are hundreds of µm (acc/res) or about 100 µm (thermal) apart.

What the fences do not separate: the clock tree, the reset tree, the shared per-lane multipliers, the A/B comparators and mismatch OR tree, the thermal voter and next-state logic, the enable/clear distribution, the ports, and the unprotected phase, out_valid_q and fault_q flip-flops (common-mode elements). These are geometric distances only, not an upset-rate model; sky130 is not radiation-characterised.
